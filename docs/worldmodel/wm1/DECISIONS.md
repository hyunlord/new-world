## Part 1

- **D1.1 Batched predictor interface.** The harness accepts `StepPredictor.step(history, ctx) -> next V row` (persistence and daily naive use it) and a batched `rollout(ep, starts, horizon, actions)`; the GRU and the ridge implement the batched form because T1 and T2-any need tens of thousands of rollouts. A unit test checks the step and batched forms agree (daily naive) and that no predictor reads rows after its start.
- **D1.2 Ridge rollout vectorized over starts.** `RidgePredictor.rollout` reproduces `bench_v0.rollout` (lags from a 24-row buffer, severity held at the start row, calendar from the episode, last-24-hour action sums) for many starts at once; W1 compares it with `bench_v0.json`.
- **D1.3 `rain_ticks_left` is reduced with max, the stocks and members with sum** (one settlement in every v0 episode, so the choice does not change any value); the presence flag is "the settlement row is not NaN".
- **D1.4 Action scaling.** Requested amounts are divided by the largest amount of the kind's B_act grid (food 120, wood 80, rain 24, cold snap 3, windfall 40): fixed constants, not fitted on any data.
- **D1.5 Fire in V predictions.** A model's `fire_lit` in a predicted V row is its Bernoulli head thresholded at 0.5 (MAE-optimal for a binary target); T3 uses the probability; T3b counts thresholded dark 02:00 rows.
- **D1.6 T2-any start set.** Every hour 00:00–23:00 of the bench's start days 3, 10, 17, … whose horizon fits, with the bench's denominator; breakdowns by target season, target-row severity and start hour.
- **D1.7 T3b.** Cases: 06:00 of one-based days 24, 52, 80, … (each winter's third day) whose fifth night ends inside the episode; truth: the number of dark 02:00 rows after the evenings of that day and the next four. Ridge features exactly as GOAL 7.4 (wood at 06:00, deadwood total, the wood drop 18:00→06:00 of the two previous nights, the deadwood change 06:00→06:00 of the two previous days, members), λ from the bench's grid on A val (MAE of predictions clipped to [0, 5]); baselines fit on A train. Splits: A test (W4), B test, OOD_v12 (no oracle, severity −1), OOD_long.
- **D1.8 T4 branches.** Both branches start from the factual episode's row at the intervention tick (the harness checks that the variant's rows ≤ that tick equal the factual's), with actions = the intervention at that row or none; c_cf_targets reads the factual rows ≤ the tick and the predicted rows after it. Cold member-hours and deaths are reconstructed from the predicted hourly differences of the cumulative tallies, starting from the factual value at the tick.
- **D1.9 T5 scored rows.** Every row t in 23 … T−2 and agent alive at t−23, t and t+1 (so the 24-hours-earlier baseline is defined); needs nMAE denominator = std of the split's scored targets.

## Part 2

- **D2.1 Training runs concurrently on the CPU.** Two to four jobs at 8 threads each share the Grace CPU (other users' load 4–11); `--threads` is recorded in every config, and the W7 rerun uses the same thread count as the run it reproduces.
- **D2.2 Recipe r1 → r2 (strike 1 on W2 and T1).** M1 main r1 (`m1gru_main_s0`, GOAL's recipe: windows of 168, k 1→24) beats persistence at 24 h on val (T2-24 0.115 vs 0.130) but drifts at 168 h (val T2-168 0.84 vs persistence 0.53: `fire_lit` and `food_on_bushes_total` reach nMAE 1.0, `wood_stock` 1.49) and loses on T1 (val 0.113 vs the ridge's ≈ 0.100; `adults_working` 0.56, `food_on_bushes_total` 0.23). Hypotheses: (1) the rollout loss never unrolls past 24 steps and starts from short contexts (j uniform in the 168-row window), so 168-step rollouts leave the training distribution; (2) the Gaussian NLL down-weights high-variance changes, which is not what an MAE metric rewards. Recipe r2: windows of 336 rows, k 1→168 over the first half, rollout starts only after a full 168-row context (`roll_min_context` 167, as in evaluation), 2 rollout starts per window, an L1 term on the predicted mean change (`tf_l1` 1.0) added to the NLL, and features that are never negative in train clamped at 0 in rollouts (`clamp_nonneg`). Early stopping, optimizer, heads and architecture unchanged. r1's runs keep their test evaluation (they are listed configurations) and are reported.
- **D2.3 r2 step budget fixed before any r2 result.** At k = 168 a step costs ≈ 1 s of one core, and with eight jobs plus other users' load (≈ 33 on 20 cores) the first r2 launch (18:47–18:58) would have hit the 2-hour wall-clock cap at a load-dependent step — making the stop point, and so the W7 same-seed rerun, nondeterministic. Those runs were stopped (moved to `wm1-runs/_aborted/`, never evaluated) and relaunched with `max_steps` 5000 and `eval_every` 250 (the curriculum reaches k = 168 at step 2,500; patience 5 evaluations = 1,250 steps). This is part of r2, not a new recipe (no r2 number existed). M3 was paused (its first launch also moved to `_aborted/`) and is relaunched when the GRU jobs finish.
- **D2.4 r2 step budget tightened again before any r2 result.** Measured step cost under the shared load: ≈ 0.45 s + 0.008 s × k (0.86 s at k = 51), i.e. ≈ 1.8 s at k = 168, so 5,000 steps would need ≈ 7,300 s — over the 2-hour cap. The 5,000-step runs (stopped at step ≈ 800, moved to `_aborted/r2_5000/`, never evaluated) were relaunched with `max_steps` 4000 and `eval_every` 200 (≈ 5,800 s estimated; k reaches 168 at step 2,000; patience 5 = 1,000 steps). The two seed-0 runs had matched exactly up to step 750 (val 0.1937, 0.1682, 0.1565 at steps 250, 500, 750), so the training loop is deterministic on this CPU.

## Part 3

- **D3.1 (superseded by D3.2) M1b (causal transformer) dropped — compute, not wall time.** On this CPU-only DGX (D0.3) one M1b training step (batch 64, 336-row window, 4 layers × d 256, k = 24 rollout) took 49 s under the shared load (the GRU: ≈ 1–2 s at k = 168), so the 2-hour cap allows fewer than 150 steps where every GRU run needed ≥ 1,200; its T1 evaluation would need one 168-row encode per start (≈ 200 k encodes over the test splits). A banded single-pass shortcut is not equivalent to per-start windows (the receptive field grows with depth), so it would have changed the model's definition. M1b is the second item of GOAL 0.3.8's drop order; it is dropped. M3 (the first item) is kept because its run had already been launched within its own 2-hour cap and costs no further wall time — dropping it would not buy M1b anything. The code (`m1_tf.py`, with its KV-cache tests) stays in the repository; the partial r1 run `m1tf_main_s0` (stopped at step ≈ 2,100, D2.2 era) was never evaluated on test.
- **D3.2 M1b reinstated on the GPU.** D3.1's timing was taken on the CPU; on the GPU (D0.3a) the M1b step costs 1.3 s at k = 24 but 11 s and 73 GB at k = 168 with batch 64 × 2 rollout starts (over the 64 GB cap). With batch 32 and one rollout start per window the k = 168 step costs 2.4 s and 19 GB, so recipe r3 (windows 336, k 1→168 from a full 168-row context, L1 + V-weight 5, non-negative clamp) fits the 2-hour cap at 3,000 steps (k = 168 from step 1,500), evaluated every 150 steps (patience 5). Run `m1tf_main_r3_s0`, main configuration, one seed, as GOAL 9 asks.
- **D3.3 M1b launch and evaluation fixes.** The first GPU launch (22:15) omitted the V-weighting of r3 (`m1_tf.py` had no `v_weight` option) and was stopped within minutes (`_aborted/m1tf_main_r3_s0_noVweight`, never evaluated); `losses_tf` now applies the same V weights as the GRU (tested), and the run was relaunched with `--v-weight 5.0`. Its first `final` crashed at model loading (`KeyError: 'm1_tf'`: `m1_gru.py` run as a script is `__main__`, a different module object from the `wm1.m1_gru` that `m1_tf` registers into) before computing any metric; after the fix (`kind_of` copies the registry) the same checkpoint was evaluated once.

## Part 4

- **D4.1 M2 architecture by validation.** Both GOAL 10 options were trained on A_obs agent episodes 0–31 (seed 0): the MLP over own + neighbour + settlement + calendar + action features (`m2_mlp_s0`, 4 epochs, 386 s) and the set transformer over all living agents of a row (2 layers, d 128, `m2_set_s0`, 2 epochs, 1,523 s). Selection score on A val (96–99) = (1 − state-tag acc) + (1 − target-kind acc) + Chebyshev / persistence Chebyshev + needs nMAE mean: MLP 1.222, set transformer 1.109 → the set transformer is M2; only it is evaluated on test.
- **D4.2 Agent "working" count.** For the aggregate check (sum over agents vs `adults_working`) an agent counts as working when alive, adult, a settlement member and its target kind is a site, gather-food or gather-wood target. Applied to the *true* agent states at t+1 this definition reaches nMAE 0.171 against the settlement column on A val (it is not the simulation's exact rule: hauling is not visible as a target kind), which bounds what the aggregate can show.
- **D2.5 Recipe r2 → r3 (strike 2 on W2, the last recipe allowed: strike 3 if it fails).** r2 fixes the 168-hour drift (main s0 val T2-168 0.483 / 0.490 vs r1 0.84 and persistence 0.527 / 0.534; the same-seed rerun is identical) but loses short horizons: val T1 0.129 / 0.131 (r1 0.113, the ridge ≈ 0.10) and T2-24 0.141 / 0.148 (r1 0.115). Per variable, T1's gap is `adults_working` (0.63; persistence 0.655, ridge 0.396) and `food_on_bushes_total` (0.26; ridge 0.177), and T2-24 loses on `adults_working` at 06:00 (0.13 where persistence is exact). Early stopping on T2-24 picked step 1,200 (k ≈ 100). Hypothesis: the losses weigh all 37 state features equally while the metric scores only the eight V variables, so the hard V counts get too little of the model. r3 = r2 + `v_weight` 5: in the teacher-forced L1 and the rollout L1 the V features weigh 5× the others (weights rescaled to mean 1), and the rollout fire BCE gets the same V weight. W2 fails under r2 on its T1 clause whatever its test T2-168 is, so r3 starts before r2's test numbers exist.

### Part 0
- **E0.1** The Mac clone carries two unrelated local changes (`.gitignore` adds `/graft/`; an untracked `.ignore`) from the owner's graft tool. They are outside the allowed paths, so they are never staged; every commit stages explicit paths only.

### Part 1
- **E1.1** The member prediction math (calendar means, wrap, α, analogs) lives in `calcore.py` as `predict_member`, so the v0 harness predictor (`climatology.CalendarPredictor`) and the v1 entry point (`diversity_v1.py`, which may not import `wm1`) share one implementation.
- **E1.2** Ties in the validation selection go to the earlier member of the order cal_abs, cal_year, ap1_abs, ap1_year, apfit_abs, apfit_year, an1, an5, an5_off. They occur only on T3 / T3b among the non-analog members (identical fire predictions), never for `cal_best`.
- **E1.3** No A_train variable has `split_std` 0, so the analog distance scales every variable by its train std (the code would use scale 1 for a zero std).
- **E1.4** `climatology.py val` evaluates every member on A_val and B_val through `evaluate.evaluate` with `t3_split="A_val"` (T3 on A_val only, GOAL_1_1.md 6.2). The final run evaluates every member on the four test splits, not only the two selected ones, so the tables can show all nine.
- **E1.5** v1's selection is recorded before any v1 calendar-family test score: `diversity_v1.py --val-only` writes the val scores and the selection; the full run (after METRICS_1_1.md) adds the test-role scores. Correction after review round 1: the `--val-only` run also computed the descriptive diagnostics of v1's test splits (amendment in METRICS_1_1.md); they are labelled as computed before pre-registration.

### Part 2
- **E2.1** η² within groups of equal winter-severity sequences pools only groups with at least two episodes (a singleton group has zero within-group variance by construction); its denominator is the same episodes' sum of squares around the split's pooled mean. The number of episodes used is reported.
- **E2.2** Divergence: pairs that never differ are counted separately and excluded from min / median / max. A_train uses 500 of its 4,560 pairs (`numpy.random.default_rng(0)`, without replacement); every other split uses all pairs (OOD_v12: 496).
