## 1. Summary

- **The calendar alone forecasts dataset v0 better than every WM-1 M1 / M1b / ridge model at 24 h and 168 h** (M3, which reads the absolute day, is the exception at 24 h: section 6). The val-selected calendar forecaster for T2 168 h is `an5`, the mean of the five A_train episodes whose last 24 hours look most like the test window, followed forward in time (Lorenz analogs). On A_test it scores **0.076** at 168 h (bench starts), against M1b 0.122 (seed 0, WM-1's run), `ridge_nosev` 0.223, the M1 GRU 0.275 (3-seed mean) and persistence 0.554. Even the plain per-row train mean (CalAbs, which never looks at the test episode) scores 0.099.
- **X2 (M1b 168 h) and X3 are not decidable as pre-registered**: seeds 1 and 2 were trained (val T2-24 0.081 and 0.067; seed 0 0.072, so seed 2 is the best of the three on val) but their test evaluations were running on the DGX when it became unreachable (about 11:20) and did not come back. On seed 0 alone (a single-seed reading, not the X2 rule), M1b's 168 h nMAE is 1.21–1.60× `cal_best` in all six X2 cells (skill −0.21 to −0.60) and 0.78–0.94× `cal_best_fair` (the best forecaster that never reads absolute time, `apfit_year` at 168 h; skill +0.06 to +0.22): for the 3-seed mean to fall below `cal_best` in the A_test 168 h cell, seeds 1 and 2 would have to average below 0.053, less than half of seed 0's 0.122. KEEP against `cal_best` is already excluded for X2 and X3 (seed 0 is above `cal_best` in those cells); only WEAK vs NONE is open. At T1 seed 0 ties `cal_best` (`an5_off`, ratio 0.99–1.01); at 24 h it is 1.08–1.17× `cal_best` and 0.91–1.02× the fair bar. X5: the severity-free ridge and the M1 GRU are **NONE** against both bars — the baselines WM-1 judged M1b against have 1.7–3.6× the nMAE of `cal_best` and 1.2–2.1× that of `cal_best_fair` in the X2 cells.
- **X4 (M3): consistent with calendar memorization.** M3's 0.056 at 24 h is 0.86× `cal_best` (`an5_off`, 0.064) — not the pre-registered 20 % below it — although it is clearly better than CalAbs (0.085). M3-noday (the same lines without the day number) was trained (276 steps) but its test evaluation was cut off by the DGX outage, so M3 − M3-noday is not available.
- **The seeds of v0 barely differ.** Every pair of episodes diverges on day 1, but on A_test the between-seed share of the variance η² is <= 0.05 for wood, fire, population and deadwood (0.085 for food), and the A_train calendar mean explains R²_abs >= 0.95 of them (X1 flags wood, fire, alive, deadwood as calendar-determined; food 0.899). Dataset v1 (game_v14) is not materially more diverse, and the calendar beats v1's ridges just as clearly.
- **Post hoc, seed 0 only (no pre-registered rule): where the calendar fails, M1b does not:** on the winter-event tasks (T3 fire-out at 02:00, T3b dark nights) the calendar family is worse than M1b (T3b A_test 0.297 for the best analog vs 0.203; T3 Brier 0.0077 vs 0.0055), because those outcomes depend on this year's severity draw and the current wood, not on the date.
- **So v0 cannot certify "learned dynamics" at 24–168 h.** A forecaster that only knows the calendar and the nearest training trajectories beats every M1 / M1b / ridge model at these horizons. Seed 0 of the transformer is better than the fair calendar (season / day-of-season / hour only) at T1 and 168 h but not consistently at 24 h (fair-bar skill −0.02 to +0.09); this is a single-seed observation, not a pre-registered result, and even if seeds 1–2 matched it, X2 against the fair bar would be WEAK, not KEEP. Whether any such margin is dynamics or trajectory matching through the 7-day context cannot be told apart on v0.

## 2. What was measured

- **Calendar family** (`climatology.py`, `calcore.py`): nine forecasters that know only what dataset v0's world
  usually looks like at a given time — the per-row A_train mean (CalAbs), the mean at the same position of the
  28-day year (CalYear), anomaly persistence with α = 1 and with α fit per lead and variable (`ap1_*`, `apfit_*`,
  each on both means), and Lorenz (1969) analogs (`an1`, `an5`, `an5_off`: the A_train episodes whose last 24 hours
  are nearest to the test window, followed forward). All are fit on A_train only, read the test episode's rows <= s
  only and ignore actions and severity (unit tests). `cal_best` / `cal_best_fair` (the best member that never reads
  absolute time: CalYear, `ap1_year`, `apfit_year`) were chosen per task on A_val + B_val and pre-registered.
- **Seed diversity** (`diversity.py`, `diversity_v1.py`): R² of the calendar means, the between-seed variance share
  η², the day two episodes first differ, the between-seed spread, and the winter-severity draws, per split.
- **M1b seeds 1 and 2** (WM-1's recipe, seed changed), **M1b sev-on** (seed 0), **M3 without the day field**
  (276 steps like M3), and the re-judging of M1b, M1 GRU, the severity-free ridges and M3 against the calendar.
- **Dataset v1** (Pass 8's game_v14): the same diversity diagnostics and calendar baselines, against `bench_v1`.

## 10. What this means for the research plan

**(a) Can v0 distinguish dynamics learning from calendar memorization?** Not at 24–168 h. In v0 every episode starts from the same calendar instant on the same scene, the 96 train seeds differ only through a few random streams, and the result is a family of trajectories whose spread is a small fraction of the seasonal swing (η² <= 0.05 for four of the eight variables on A_test, 0.085 for food). A forecaster that copies the nearest training trajectories (analogs) already beats the transformer (seed 0), the GRU and the ridges at 24 h and 168 h, and even the train mean (CalAbs) beats seed 0 of the transformer at 168 h from the bench starts, and the rule X2 cannot return KEEP against such a bar for any model whose errors are not far below 0.076. The only bar v0 can support is the fair calendar (no absolute time): against it seed 0 of M1b shows modest skill at 168 h (≈ 0.06–0.22; single seed, not a pre-registered result), and part of any such margin could be trajectory matching through the 7-day context rather than mechanism.

**(b) What diversity would a dataset v2 need?** For X2 to be informative, the calendar-family error must be large relative to a dynamics model's error. Two levers: (1) **between-seed variance** — η² of the stock and population variables should be at least ≈ 0.3–0.5 (a heuristic target, not derived or tested here) (cold members already have 0.6 because of the severity draws; food 0.09, wood 0.05, alive 0.02 today), so that at least a third of the variance is not explained by the calendar; equivalently (also heuristic) R²_abs of the calendar mean should fall below ≈ 0.5–0.7 on test. (2) **early, persistent divergence** — trajectories should differ by a large fraction of the split std within the first weeks (today the between-seed spread at 06:00 is 0.1–0.3 split std on days 30–118). Pass 9–10's plan (keyed randomness per subsystem, forks of one state under different draws, resampled latents and varied start states / start calendar instants) addresses both; varied start instants alone would also break the "row r = same calendar instant" property that the analogs and CalAbs exploit. v1 (game_v14) roughly doubled the food diversity (η² 0.195) but left the other stocks at <= 0.06, and the calendar still beats its ridges by about 2×, so v2 needs structural, not cosmetic, randomness. Fork datasets (same state, different future draws) would additionally give a direct measure of irreducible spread to judge forecasts against.

**(c) Which WM-1 statements must be qualified?** "M1b beats the best baseline by ≈ 45 % at 168 h" (W2 against `ridge_nosev`) remains true as stated but is not evidence of learned dynamics: the val-selected calendar forecaster (`an5`) has a 30–38 % lower 168 h nMAE than M1b seed 0 on A_test, B_test and OOD_long. "M3 forecasts 24 h with nMAE 0.056" must be read with its absolute-day input; it is consistent with calendar memorization by the pre-registered rule. WM-1's statements that no model learned intervention effects (T4) and that M1b is worse than the observable ridge on the winter outlook (T3b) stand. The calendar family adds one point in M1b's favour: on the event tasks that depend on this winter's draw it is *better* than every calendar member (T3b MAE on A_test 0.203 vs `an5` 0.297 and 0.844 for the pure calendar means; T3 Brier 0.0055 vs 0.0077), which, post hoc and on seed 0 only, is the clearest sign in v0 that the transformer uses state rather than date.

## 11. Limitations

- One val selection per task; the analog members are deterministic given A_train, so the calendar numbers carry no seed spread; M1b has a test number for seed 0 only.
- The analogs and CalAbs read absolute time (the same row of other episodes); M1/M1b cannot. `cal_best_fair` (no absolute time) is the fair bar for them; the verdicts are reported against both.
- M1b reproducibility on the GPU was not measured (WM-1 nor here); seed 0 is WM-1's run, seeds 1 and 2 trained under the same recipe (seed 1 stopped by patience at step 2,100, best 1,350).
- M3 and M3-noday are one seed each, 39 % of one epoch (276 steps), CPU; M3's 24 h number uses day-granularity lines and rounded values; M3-noday has no test number.
- The diversity diagnostics are descriptive; η² within severity groups uses only groups of at least two episodes (few on the test splits).
- The DGX became unreachable at about 11:20 and was still unreachable when this report was finished (DECISIONS E4.1, E4.3). Four jobs were lost and not re-run: the test evaluations of M1b seeds 1 and 2, the test evaluation of M3-noday, and the M1b sev-on training. Hence X2, X3 and X6 are not decidable, M3 − M3-noday is missing, and the DGX half of the gate did not run.
- Nothing here re-evaluates WM-1's runs; their `metrics.json` files are read as they are.
