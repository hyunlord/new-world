## 1. Summary

- **The calendar alone forecasts dataset v0 better than every WM-1 M1 / M1b / ridge model at 24 h and 168 h** (M3, which reads the absolute day, is the exception at 24 h: section 6). The val-selected calendar forecaster for T2 168 h is `an5`, the mean of the five A_train episodes whose last 24 hours look most like the test window, followed forward in time (Lorenz analogs). On A_test it scores **0.076** at 168 h (bench starts), against M1b 0.122 / 0.120 / 0.108 (seeds 0 / 1 / 2, mean 0.116), `ridge_nosev` 0.223, the M1 GRU 0.275 (3-seed mean) and persistence 0.554. Even the plain per-row train mean (CalAbs, which never looks at the test episode) scores 0.099.
- **X2 (M1b, 168 h, three seeds): NONE against `cal_best`, WEAK against `cal_best_fair`.** The 3-seed mean is 1.21–1.53× `cal_best` in the six cells (every seed above it), and 0.74–0.92× the fair calendar (the best forecaster that never reads absolute time, `apfit_year`), but only the two OOD_long cells reach the 0.8× needed for KEEP. **X3 (T1 and 24 h): NONE against both bars** — at T1 the mean ties `cal_best` (`an5_off`, 1.00–1.01×) and at 24 h it is 1.07–1.16×; against the fair bar the B_test 24 h cells are at 1.00×. **X5:** the severity-free ridge and the M1 GRU are **NONE** against both bars, with 1.7–3.6× the nMAE of `cal_best` and 1.2–2.1× that of `cal_best_fair` in the X2 cells.
- **X4 (M3): consistent with calendar memorization, with or without the day field.** M3's 0.0558 at 24 h is 0.86× `cal_best` (`an5_off`, 0.0645) and M3-noday's 0.0550 is 0.85×; neither is the pre-registered 20 % below it. **M3 − M3-noday = +0.0008**: removing the absolute day changed nothing (per variable −0.014 to +0.012), so M3's skill does not come from the day number; the line's season and day-of-season plus the current state carry it.
- **The seeds of v0 barely differ.** Every pair of episodes diverges on day 1, but on A_test the between-seed share of the variance η² is <= 0.05 for wood, fire, population and deadwood (0.085 for food), and the A_train calendar mean explains R²_abs >= 0.95 of them (X1 flags wood, fire, alive, deadwood as calendar-determined; food 0.899). Dataset v1 (game_v14) roughly doubles the food diversity and changes little else, and the calendar beats v1's ridges just as clearly.
- **Post hoc (no pre-registered rule): where the calendar fails, M1b mostly does not.** On T3 (fire out at 02:00) all three seeds beat every calendar member (Brier 0.0055 / 0.0045 / 0.0036 vs 0.0077 for `an5`); on T3b (dark nights) all three beat the best analog on A_test (0.203 / 0.234 / 0.266 vs 0.297) and OOD_long, while on B_test and OOD_v12 seed 2 is worse. These outcomes depend on this year's severity draw and the current wood, not on the date.
- **So v0 cannot certify "learned dynamics" at 24–168 h.** A forecaster that only knows the calendar and the nearest training trajectories beats every M1 / M1b / ridge model at these horizons. The transformer is better than the fair calendar (season / day-of-season / hour only) at 168 h (WEAK by the pre-registered rule, KEEP only on OOD_long), i.e. it extracts more from its 7-day context than the year position does; whether that margin is dynamics or trajectory matching cannot be told apart on v0.

## 2. What was measured

- **Calendar family** (`climatology.py`, `calcore.py`): nine forecasters that know only what dataset v0's world
  usually looks like at a given time — the per-row A_train mean (CalAbs), the mean at the same position of the
  28-day year (CalYear), anomaly persistence with α = 1 and with α fit per lead and variable (`ap1_*`, `apfit_*`,
  each on both means), and Lorenz (1969) analogs (`an1`, `an5`, `an5_off`: the A_train episodes whose last 24 hours
  are nearest to the test window, followed forward). All are fit on A_train only, read the test episode's rows <= s
  only and ignore actions and severity (unit tests). `cal_best` / `cal_best_fair` (the best member that never reads
  absolute time: CalYear, `ap1_year`, `apfit_year`) were chosen per task on A_val + B_val and pre-registered.
- **Seed diversity** (`diversity.py`, `diversity_v1.py`): R² of the calendar means, the between-seed variance share
  η², the day two episodes first differ, the between-seed spread, and the winter-severity draws, per split.
- **M1b seeds 1 and 2** (WM-1's recipe, seed changed), **M1b sev-on** (seed 0), **M3 without the day field**
  (276 steps like M3), and the re-judging of M1b, M1 GRU, the severity-free ridges and M3 against the calendar.
- **Dataset v1** (Pass 8's game_v14): the same diversity diagnostics and calendar baselines, against `bench_v1`.

## 10. What this means for the research plan

**(a) Can v0 distinguish dynamics learning from calendar memorization?** Not at 24–168 h. In v0 every episode starts from the same calendar instant on the same scene, the 96 train seeds differ only through a few random streams, and the result is a family of trajectories whose spread is a small fraction of the seasonal swing (η² <= 0.05 for four of the eight variables on A_test, 0.085 for food). A forecaster that copies the nearest training trajectories (analogs) already beats the transformer (all three seeds), the GRU and the ridges at 24 h and 168 h, and even the train mean (CalAbs, 0.099) beats every transformer seed at 168 h from the bench starts on A_test; the rule X2 cannot return KEEP against such a bar for any model whose errors are not far below 0.076. The only bar v0 can support is the fair calendar (no absolute time): against it M1b's three seeds show modest skill at 168 h (mean 0.74–0.92× the fair calendar: X2 = WEAK against `cal_best_fair`, KEEP only in the OOD_long cells), and part of that margin could be trajectory matching through the 7-day context rather than mechanism.

**(b) What diversity would a dataset v2 need?** For X2 to be informative, the calendar-family error must be large relative to a dynamics model's error. Two levers: (1) **between-seed variance** — η² of the stock and population variables should be at least ≈ 0.3–0.5 (a heuristic target, not derived or tested here; cold members already have 0.6 because of the severity draws; food 0.09, wood 0.05, alive 0.02 today), so that at least a third of the variance is not explained by the calendar; equivalently (also heuristic) R²_abs of the calendar mean should fall below ≈ 0.5–0.7 on test. (2) **early, persistent divergence** — trajectories should differ by a large fraction of the split std within the first weeks (today the between-seed spread at 06:00 is 0.1–0.3 split std on days 30–118). Pass 9–10's plan (keyed randomness per subsystem, forks of one state under different draws, resampled latents and varied start states / start calendar instants) addresses both; varied start instants alone would also break the "row r = same calendar instant" property that the analogs and CalAbs exploit. v1 (game_v14) roughly doubled the food diversity (η² 0.195) but left the other stocks at <= 0.06, and the calendar still beats its ridges by about 2×, so v2 needs structural, not cosmetic, randomness. Fork datasets (same state, different future draws) would additionally give a direct measure of irreducible spread to judge forecasts against.

**(c) Which WM-1 statements must be qualified?** "M1b beats the best baseline by ≈ 45 % at 168 h" (W2 against `ridge_nosev`) remains true as stated but is not evidence of learned dynamics: the val-selected calendar forecaster (`an5`) has a 26–35 % lower 168 h nMAE than the 3-seed M1b mean on A_test, B_test and OOD_long (X2 = NONE). "M3 forecasts 24 h with nMAE 0.056" is consistent with calendar memorization by the pre-registered rule, but not because of the absolute day: M3-noday scores the same (0.055). WM-1's statements that no model learned intervention effects (T4) and that M1b is worse than the observable ridge on the winter outlook (T3b) stand. The calendar family adds one point in M1b's favour (post hoc): on the event tasks that depend on this winter's draw it is mostly *better* than every calendar member (T3 Brier 0.0036–0.0055 for the three seeds vs 0.0077 for `an5`; T3b MAE on A_test 0.203–0.266 vs 0.297, and 0.844 for the pure calendar means; seed 2 is worse than `an5` on B_test and OOD_v12), the clearest sign in v0 that the transformer uses state rather than date.

## 11. Limitations

- One val selection per task; the analog members are deterministic given A_train, so the calendar numbers carry no seed spread, while M1b has three seeds.
- The analogs and CalAbs read absolute time (the same row of other episodes); M1/M1b cannot. `cal_best_fair` (no absolute time) is the fair bar for them; the verdicts are reported against both.
- M1b reproducibility on the GPU was not measured (WM-1 nor here); seed 0 is WM-1's run, seeds 1 and 2 trained under the same recipe (seed 1 stopped by patience at step 2,100, best 1,350).
- M3 and M3-noday are one seed each, 39 % of one epoch (276 steps), CPU; their 24 h numbers use day-granularity lines and rounded values.
- The diversity diagnostics are descriptive; η² within severity groups uses only groups of at least two episodes (few on the test splits).
- M1b sev-on was dropped (X6 empty) under drop order 0.3.8.
- From about 11:20 to 20:25 the Mac could not reach the DGX (a Tailscale path failure, not a DGX failure: no reboot, OOM or kernel error; DECISIONS E7.1). The three test evaluations then running finished normally at 11:27 and 11:54 from the pre-registration commit and are used as they are (E7.2); an earlier version of this document wrongly called them lost.
- Nothing here re-evaluates WM-1's runs; their `metrics.json` files are read as they are.
