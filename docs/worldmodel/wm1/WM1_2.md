# WM-1.2: a harness for the new worlds, and a first look — does a learned model beat the calendar when worlds differ?

Run of `docs/worldmodel/wm1/GOAL_1_2.md` on `codex/worldmodel-2026-10-wip` (base `d5380301`, WM-1.1 final), after merging
Pass 10's green head `14372ea2`. Data: the Pass 10 pilot (`p10/pilot`, recorder schema 4, 112 worlds per scene, 120
days; train 20000–20079, val 20080–20095, test 20096–20111). Pre-registration: `METRICS_1_2.md` (commit `1a7dfdb6`,
2026-10-12 21:59:00, before every test number below). Verdicts and tables come from `tools/worldmodel/wm1/judge_1_2.py`
(`runs/judge_1_2/`), which reads only the result files under `runs/`.

Scores are nMAE (MAE / the std of the variable over rows ≥ 24 of the test split; lower is better), mean over the 8 V
variables. **Every-hour starts** (every hour of days 3, 10, 17, …) are primary; 06:00 starts are reported. The
**bias-removed** score subtracts, per world and variable, the predictor's mean error over that world's scored starts
before taking the nMAE: what remains is what the predictor gets wrong about how the world *moves*, not about its
*level*. `cal_best` / `cal_best_fair` / `cal_best_anom` are the WM-1.1 calendar family's members chosen on val
(METRICS_1_2.md section 3); `apfit_abs` is damped anomaly persistence on the absolute calendar; the ridge is
`bench_v1`'s observable ridge refit on the pilot's 80 training worlds.

Reading the rows: **Z1 NONE** — on `game_v16` the 3-seed M1b mean at 168 h (every-hour starts) is 0.221 against the
analog forecast `an5`'s 0.206; no seed is below it. **Z1b WEAK** — at 24 h the model is ahead (0.160 against
`an5_off`'s 0.167, every seed below; ratio 0.96, while KEEP would need ≤ 0.134). **Z2 NONE** — with each
world's static offset removed, the model (0.223) is behind the calendar mean `cal_abs` (0.211). **Z3 NONE** as
expected — on `game_v15` the model (0.209) is far behind `an5` (0.138).

Z4 says where the 168 h numbers come from. The model's `alive` error (0.080) is exactly persistence's (0.080 in
every seed): it holds the population at its last value, which is the right call in worlds whose population is set
mostly at worldgen, and `an5` (0.156), which averages five other worlds, cannot match it. Once each world's level is
removed, the same choice is worse than both baselines (0.123 against `cal_abs`'s 0.064 and `apfit_abs`'s 0.114: the calendar mean carries the population's
seasonal movement, persistence does not). On food the model beats `apfit_abs` (skill 0.22 raw, 0.27 bias-removed)
and `cal_abs` bias-removed (0.10), but loses to `an5` raw (−0.15). Bushes and working adults are close to every
baseline.

Z5: the model's state on day 8 at 06:00 carries the world's identity. A linear probe recovers `starting_agents`
(R² 0.998 in every seed), the bush count (0.89) and bush capacity (0.82), the deadwood spot count (0.51) and part of
food regrowth (0.31); it does not recover deadwood capacity (−0.02), the forest share (−0.09) or the climate class
(accuracy 0.52 against 0.375 for the train-majority class, 16 test worlds). The same ridge on the raw observable
row at the same hour gets 0.83 / 0.76 / 0.81 / 0.52 / 0.25 and climate 0.375: the model's window adds to what the
current row shows for the population and the bush count, not for the other parameters. (`starting_agents` equals
`alive` at row 0; by day 8 births and deaths have moved `alive`, so the raw row recovers it at 0.83, not 1.)

## What this means for dataset v2 and WM-2

**(a) Do hidden parameters alone give a learned model an edge over the calendar family, and is it identification or
forecasting?** Not at 168 h, on 80 training worlds. World variety hurts every forecaster that borrows from other
worlds (analogs, calendar means, anomaly persistence: +50–60 % error) and barely hurts the model (+6 %), but the
analogs, which also read the episode's own last 24 hours, stay ahead (Z1 NONE). What the model gains is
identification: its state identifies the population size and the bush stock almost perfectly (Z5), and at 168 h it
turns that into holding the population and a better food level (Z4 raw). With the per-world level removed it does
not forecast the movement better than the calendar mean (Z2 NONE; `alive` is the clearest case). At 24 h it is
ahead on both scores (raw 0.160 vs 0.167, WEAK; bias-removed 0.163 vs `an5_off`'s 0.169, reported only), so there
is some short-range dynamic skill, but it does not carry to a week.

**(b) What WM-2's benchmark must report by default.** Raw and bias-removed scores side by side, every-hour starts
primary, at 24 h and 168 h, per variable; `cal_best` (and its bias-removed counterpart `cal_best_anom`) chosen on
val, `cal_best_fair`, `apfit_abs`, persistence and the observable ridge, all through one harness and one split std.
Persistence must stay in the table: in hidden-parameter worlds it is the best predictor of a static variable
(`alive`) at 168 h, and a model that matches it there is identifying, not forecasting. A per-world identification
probe (state → hidden parameters, with the raw-row reference) should accompany any model claim, and the family
evaluator (`wm1/families.py`: replicate-distribution scoring, home-slot dark nights, exposure deaths) is ready for
dataset v2's branch families.

**(c) Are 80 training worlds enough?** Not shown to be. Two of the three `game_v16` seeds were still improving
when the 3,000-step cap ended them (best at step 2,850; val T2 24 h fell from ≈ 0.107 at step 1,500 to ≈ 0.101–0.105),
the third stopped on patience with a flat curve, and the 168 h val score (0.229–0.231) sits near `an5`'s 0.238 while
the test score is behind (0.221 vs 0.206): with 16 val and 16 test worlds, a 0.01–0.02 difference at 168 h is within
what the choice of worlds moves. WM-2 should train on more worlds (or longer, with a step cap that does not bind)
and report a world-level spread (e.g. a bootstrap over test worlds) beside each score.

## Limitations

- 80 / 16 / 16 worlds; one pilot recording; no interventions (T3, T3b, T4 not run; models action-blind).
- GPU reproducibility of M1b is not measured (as in WM-1.1); M1b seeds 0–1 were trained from `da379c17` and seed 2
  and the v15 control from `1a7dfdb6`; the two commits differ only in files the training does not read
  (`M1TF.hidden`, the probe, the judge, METRICS_1_2.md).
- The bias-removed score subtracts the mean error, so for a skewed error it can exceed the raw score (persistence's
  does); it is defined per world over that world's scored starts (16 starts at 06:00, 384 every-hour at 168 h).
- The probe is linear and fit on 80 worlds; R² is on 16 test worlds, with no interval.
- The family evaluator was run on real data only as a smoke test (2 root seeds × 2 variants × 4 replicates;
  persistence and zero effect): no model claim.
