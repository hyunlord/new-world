## 1. Summary

- **The benchmark now has the setting the field's methods were built for.** A confounded observational split of
  `game_v19` (560 worlds, 2,219 episodes): a god casts at most once, gifts to hungry worlds and spoils to fed ones by a
  known, pre-registered policy (among casts in the hungry tercile 86 % are gifts, in the fed tercile 73 % spoils), with
  counterfactual families (`none` and all three arms) at each val / test world's decision row. Every observed episode
  equals its factual through the cast row and its family member after it.
- **Q1 (primary): no.** On the obs test neither CRN nor the Causal Transformer beats "no effect": O1 (7 d) CRN-obs 0.497
  and CT-obs 0.321 vs `zero` 0.218, both **NONE**; M1b-obs 0.214, **TIE**. O2 (30 d) all three **NONE** (0.863, 0.373,
  0.175 vs 0.168). M1b-obs ignores the action; CRN and CT respond to it in the confounded direction (a gift's 7-day
  food effect +79–95 / +38–50 vs true +18.6). The observational estimators show the confounding plainly (O4: `naive`
  +0.381 worse than `zero` at 7 d, `ipw` +0.173; weighting removes more than half of the excess) but no pooled
  estimator beats `zero`, which `zero` won on the val families too.
- **Q2 (counterfactual data): mixed.** WM-3's `wm3_full`, trained on v2's paired branches of other worlds, beats CRN-obs
  and CT-obs at both lags (O3 **KEEP** × 4) but not M1b-obs (7 d **NONE**, +0.015; 30 d **TIE**), and WM-2's unpaired
  M1b-act scores the same as M1b-obs on this split. The KEEPs reflect how poorly CRN and CT do, not a gain from pairs.
  Paired training does teach the size of a spoil's immediate food loss (−38 / −76 / −83 vs true −81) that no
  obs-trained model gets.
- **Q3 (v2): the field's methods stand last.** B1 (7 d) CRN-v2 1.474 and CT-v2 1.007 vs `an5_arm` 0.485 and
  `wm3_full` 0.496: **NONE** × 4; B2 (30 d) 1.547 and 1.374 vs `zero` 0.487: **NONE** × 2. Most of the gap is spurious
  death effects from two rollouts that drift apart (post hoc: the three deaths cells average 1.4–4.2 for CRN / CT vs
  0.3–0.6 for the others), but without them CRN and CT still trail `an5_arm` at 7 days and `zero` at 30.
- **Q4a (ablation, three seeds): yes at 7 days, no at 30.** A1 `wm3_full` vs `wm3_ctrl` −0.075 [−0.100, −0.047] and vs
  `wm3_os` −0.068 [−0.091, −0.046] at 7 days (**WEAK** × 2: real, not ≥ 20 %); at 30 days **NONE** (+0.010) and **TIE**.
  The 7-day gain is the pair construction's.
- **Q4b (hazard head, selected for calibration): calibrated, not informative.** Platt recalibration on train-family
  rollouts brings the test Brier from 0.39–0.65 to 0.218–0.221 (climatology 0.224), but H1's split timing is still
  **NONE** against `ridge_day` (36.0 vs 29.2 days; WM-3 V4 46.0) and the split shift **TIE** with `an5_arm` (10.8 vs 11.4).
- **Caveat that bounds Q1 / Q3:** CRN and CT ran under pre-registered 20 / 40-minute budgets; the kept CT checkpoints
  have 477–493 optimizer steps (4 % of the authors' 11,850) with the domain-confusion weight at 20 % of its maximum. The
  results are for these methods at this budget (METHODS D14).

## 2. The observational split (Part 1)

**Why.** In dataset v2 every arm is cast on every world by a fixed rule: assignment does not depend on the state, so
the methods whose contribution is correcting time-varying confounding (CRN's balancing representation, CT's domain
confusion) have nothing to correct. Their own benchmarks (the tumour-growth simulator with confounding strength γ) train
on observational data whose treatments depend on the state and test on counterfactual truth. The recorder can produce
exactly that, because a world's trajectory up to a cast is identical with and without the cast.

**The policy** (METRICS.md part a, frozen before any treated episode): a god reads the factual trajectory at 06:00 of
days 8 … 60 and casts at most once. Food per person below the 33rd train percentile (8.08) makes a gift 15 × as likely
as a spoil (0.030 vs 0.002 per day); above the 67th (12.1) the reverse; `rot_wood` is 3 × as likely in the top wood
tercile. 560 worlds (400 train, 80 val, 80 test), 2,219 recorded episodes (560 factual, 1,019 observed, 640 family members) (`DATASET_OBS.md`).

**Realized.** 18.0 % of worlds never cast; casts are 39.9 % gift, 40.5 % spoil, 19.6 % rot (GOAL.md expected
17–19 % and 40 / 40 / 20). **The confounding is visible:** among casts in the hungry tercile 85.8 % are gifts and 3.4 %
spoils; in the fed tercile 6.9 % gifts and 73.3 % spoils. Overlap holds by construction (every arm ≥ 0.002 per day in
every state), but the weights are extreme: 129 / 132 / 59 treated train units carry an IPW effective sample size of
44 / 55 / 47 (weights up to 500).

**Checks.** Every observed episode equals the factual through its cast row (array for array); every family's
`none_r0` equals the factual and a casting world's observed episode equals its family member of the same arm (per-array
sha256); 160 / 160 families pass WM-2's prefix check. 3 of 459 casts were `rot_wood` on an empty store (no change).

**What the obs test asks.** 80 test worlds × 3 arms at the world's reference row (its cast row, or day 30): 240 cases,
12 cells per lag. The truth shows the same 30-day sign reversal as v2: a gift's food effect is +18.6 at 7 days and
−16.9 at 30; a spoil's −81.4 and +32.4.

## 3. Methods and runs (Parts 2 and 4)

**Baselines on the obs split** (`effects4.py`, observed train episodes and `policy.json` only): `zero`; `naive`
(treated minus control mean change over every decision row); `ipw` (Hájek-weighted: 1 / p_a(c) for the treated,
1 / ∏(1 − p_tot) over [c, c + h) for the controls; the mean over risk-set pairs of E[Δy_a − Δy_none]); `match5`
(5 nearest treated and 5 nearest same-day controls by an5's 24-row distance). The unit test fails if the weights are
wrong: under a synthetic confounded policy `naive` is biased by more than 60 and `ipw` is within 15 of the true +100.
**Chosen on the val families: `zero` at both lags** (12-cell val averages 0.208 / 0.209 vs `ipw` 0.397 / 0.420, `naive`
0.576 / 0.354, `match5` 0.99 / 1.08); the same choice with the unfloored scales.

**CRN and CT** (`causal.py`, the authors' plain-PyTorch modules from `Valentyn1997/CausalTransformer` `c49a24fa`, MIT,
vendored unchanged; every deviation in `METHODS.md`): daily tokens (a token every 24 rows; the cast falls in one token),
37 state features as outcomes (normalized daily changes, fed back), the calendar as history-only covariates, a
4-class treatment plus amount. CRN: LSTM encoder with a gradient-reversal treatment classifier, then a decoder
initialized from the encoder's representation, trained on 29-step windows. CT: three-stream transformer with
counterfactual domain confusion and EMA weights. Selection on validation factual loss only (30-token factual rollouts).
Grids (4 configurations × 20 min on `obs_v19`): CRN chose BR 64 with dropout 0.1 (`val_roll` 0.413), CT chose lr 1e-3
with BR 64 (0.273). **Both are undertrained relative to the authors**: the kept CT-obs checkpoint has 477 optimizer
steps (4 % of the authors' 11,850) with the domain-confusion weight at 20 % of its maximum; CRN's kept encoder / decoder
have 1,878 / 8,435 steps with the reversal weight at 0.53 / 0.65 (METHODS D14). These runs therefore test the methods'
architectures and recipes under a 20-minute budget, not domain confusion or gradient reversal as such.

**M1b-obs** (`m1_obs.py`): WM-2's M1b-act recipe from scratch on the 400 observed train episodes, selection on val
factual T2 24 h. `s0` stopped on patience (kept step 4,650), `s1` at 8,000 steps (kept 7,800: not converged).
Factual T2 on the obs test (raw, every hour): 24 h 0.113 / 0.112, 168 h 0.173 / 0.169.

## 5. Q1 — on confounded observational data, do CRN and CT beat simple baselines and M1?

**No.** On the obs test (80 worlds × 3 arms, 12 cells per lag, floored scales), the three obs-trained methods against
`zero` (the val-chosen baseline):

| method (seeds) | 7 d (seeds) | Δ vs `zero` 0.218 [CI] | O1 | 30 d (seeds) | Δ vs `zero` 0.168 [CI] | O2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CRN-obs | 0.497 (0.464, 0.530) | +0.279 [+0.245, +0.316] | NONE | 0.863 (0.686, 1.041) | +0.695 [+0.617, +0.789] | NONE |
| CT-obs | 0.321 (0.296, 0.346) | +0.103 [+0.079, +0.128] | NONE | 0.373 (0.368, 0.378) | +0.205 [+0.186, +0.225] | NONE |
| M1b-obs | 0.214 (0.213, 0.215) | −0.004 [−0.008, +0.001] | TIE | 0.175 (0.175, 0.175) | +0.007 [+0.003, +0.011] | NONE |

The unfloored scales give the same verdicts (7 d: CRN 2.48, CT 2.00, M1b 1.14 vs `zero` 1.12; 30 d: 2.36, 0.81, 0.23 vs
0.21). **Two failure modes, one shared cause.** M1b-obs ignores the god's hand, as WM-2's models did: its mean
predicted 7-day food effects are −5.7 / −6.8 for a gift (true +18.6) and −8.7 / −16.0 for a spoil (true −81.4), so it
lands next to `zero`. CRN and CT respond to the action, in the right direction at 7 days, but too strongly and in the
direction of the confounding: a gift +79 / +95 (CRN) and +38 / +50 (CT) against +18.6, a spoil −138 / −126 and −112 /
−94 against −81. `naive`, which ignores the confounding entirely, says +139 and −232 — the methods sit between the truth
and the confounded contrast. They also miss the 30-day reversal (a spoil's food effect is +32 at 30 days; CRN and CT
predict −20 to −28), and their two rollouts drift apart in outcomes the actions do not touch (a gift's 30-day deaths:
true +0.03, CRN +2.9). With 320 casts among 48,000 train tokens, an action is seen at most once per sequence; the
balancing representations had little room (D14), and the outcome heads learned the confounded association.

**Confounding is visible in the simple estimators (O4).** On the obs test, `naive` is worse than `zero` by +0.381
[+0.336, +0.423] at 7 days, `ipw` by +0.173 [+0.146, +0.200]: weighting removes more than half of naive's excess error
(ipw − naive −0.209 [−0.231, −0.182]) — the policy's propensities do correct the confounding — but a pooled average
effect cannot beat `zero` when the per-world effects vary this much (gift 7-day food effect: mean 18.6, std 43.5). At 30
days the order flips (`ipw` − `naive` +0.059 [+0.052, +0.066]): with h = 720 the control weights grow and the treated
ESS is 44–55 of 59–132 treated units. `match5` is the worst (0.945 / 0.968): five neighbours are too few for effects
this noisy.

WM-3's `wm3_full` (trained on v2's paired branches of other worlds, never on obs data) against each obs-trained model:

| comparison | 7 d: `wm3_full` vs model [CI] | O3 | 30 d [CI] | O3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| vs CRN-obs | 0.229 vs 0.497, −0.268 [−0.304, −0.236] | KEEP | 0.175 vs 0.863, −0.688 [−0.781, −0.610] | KEEP |
| vs CT-obs | 0.229 vs 0.321, −0.092 [−0.111, −0.074] | KEEP | 0.175 vs 0.373, −0.198 [−0.218, −0.179] | KEEP |
| vs M1b-obs | 0.229 vs 0.214, +0.015 [+0.001, +0.029] | NONE | 0.175 vs 0.175, +0.000 [−0.004, +0.004] | TIE |

**Read with care (review round 1, M2).** The KEEPs over CRN-obs and CT-obs say how badly those two do (both are worse
than `zero`), not that paired branches help: `wm3_full` differs from the obs-trained models in data volume (≈ 3,800 vs
400 episodes), worlds, initialization (WM-2's M1b-act) and the pair loss at once. The cleaner contrast on this split is
WM-2's own M1b-act (v2 data, no pairs), evaluated beside it: 0.216 / 0.219 / 0.213 at 7 days and 0.183 / 0.169 / 0.172
at 30 — the same as M1b-obs (0.214 / 0.175) and as `zero`. **On the obs test no model trained on any data beats
`zero`.** The one place paired training shows is the spoil's 7-day food effect: `wm3_full` predicts −38 / −76 / −83
against the true −81.4, every other model −3 to −16 or an overshoot; but it pays elsewhere (its rot-wood food effect
−26 to −47 against −4), so its 12-cell average does not move. Q2's answer: counterfactual (paired) data teaches the
size of the immediate food effect, which observational training on this split does not; it does not, by itself, beat
predicting no effect on this split's cells.

CT-v2's kept checkpoints are at step 493 of ≈ 2,700 run in both seeds (α 0.0019), selected because their factual
rollout loss was lowest. **Where they stand:** on v2, below every baseline and every M1 model of WM-2 / WM-3; their
factual 30-token rollouts on the test worlds are not bad (CT-v2 0.233 / 0.240, CRN-v2 0.298 / 0.278, in train-std units),
so the failure is the counterfactual contrast, not the factual forecast.

more-training control and cast windows without the paired loss, `wm3_full` is better at 7 days with intervals well

−0.075 with three seeds; cast windows alone (`os`) recover some of it on one seed (`os_s2` 0.508) but not on average.
At 30 days the pair construction does not help (and is 0.010 worse than the control), as WM-3 found on one seed.
Factual T2 168 h of the new seeds: `ctrl_s1` 0.166, `ctrl_s2` 0.158, `os_s1` 0.171, `os_s2` 0.171 (raw, test).

## 9. The hazard head, selected for (H1, closing WM-3's open item b)

0.75–0.99 predicted vs ≈ 0.34–0.41 observed). WM-4 recalibrated each head by a Platt map fitted on 745 frozen-trunk
720-step zero-action rollouts from the train families' cast rows (the states the head actually sees at prediction,
not the teacher-forced ones it was trained on), written into the head's weights (`wm4_haz_s*`). All three passed the
keep rule (val Brier below the original):

## 10. Design caveats

- **CRN and CT are undertrained relative to the authors** (METHODS D14): within the pre-registered 20 / 40-minute
  budgets the kept CT checkpoints have 477–493 of the authors' 11,850 optimizer steps (4 %) and a domain-confusion weight at
  20 % of its maximum; CRN's reversal weight is 0.5–0.7 at its kept checkpoints. Their results are for the methods under this
  budget; a schedule fitted to the budget would be a post-hoc run.
- **Daily resolution** (METHODS D1–D3): CRN and CT see one token per day and 37 outcomes; the M1 models see every hour.
  Effects are read at the same rows (c + 168, c + 720), but the methods' factual losses are not comparable, which is
  why O3 makes no pick among the obs-trained models.
- **The obs split is small**: 400 train worlds, 320 casts, an IPW effective sample size of 44–55 per arm. A larger
  split (or several casts per world) would give the balancing methods more to work with.
- **One reference row per world**: obs cases sit at the policy's cast row (or day 30), so the test cases inherit the
  policy's timing — gifts are evaluated where gifts are likely. This is the design (the counterfactual question asked
  where the god acted), not a defect, but it differs from v2's fixed-rule cases.
- **Floored scales**: the obs 12-cell average uses GOAL.md 4.1's floor; cells where an arm's effect is almost always zero
  (e.g. `gf240` deaths 7 d) contribute nearly nothing; the unfloored variant (reported) agrees on every O1 / O2 verdict.
- **Val counterfactuals were read once outside baseline selection** (DECISIONS L2.5): a dry run of the code paths; no
  selection or setting used them.
- **`wm3_os_s1` is WM-2's checkpoint** (its kept step is the init); A1's averaged `os` includes it.
- **No rounding of counts for CRN / CT** (D13): spurious fractional death differences between the two rollouts are penalized hard by v2's tiny deaths scales (section 7); the M1 models round counts (WM-2's val choice).
- **CT's EMA** is used for evaluation only (D9), and the vendored attention is slow (≈ 0.8 s per step), which is what
  limited CT's steps.

## 11. The paper

**Title (working).** *WorldSim-Interventions: a benchmark for counterfactual prediction under a god's hand — paired
interventional branches and a confounded observational split from one frozen simulator.*

**The benchmark.**
1. *Interventional split (dataset v2, WM-2):* 560 `game_v19` worlds; every arm cast on every world at a rule-fixed time
   (no confounding), each world forked into paired branches (factual and four arms, two replicates sharing the prefix),
   a noise floor (replicates of the factual) that bounds the irreducible effect variance. Truth: per-world effects at
   7 and 30 days and the split-day shift.
2. *Observational split (WM-4):* 560 more worlds with one cast per world drawn by a known, state-dependent policy
   (propensities recorded), observed episodes for training, and counterfactual families at the cast row of every
   val / test world. Same simulator, same outcomes, same harness — the only change is who decides when to act.
3. Code, manifests, per-array hashes and the frozen recorder; every number regenerable from committed files.

**Tasks and metrics.** Per-world treatment effects of three interventions on four outcomes at 7 and 30 days (12 cells
per lag), scaled MAE averaged over cells, world-level paired bootstrap; the split-day shift and split timing as
secondary tasks; factual T2 as a guard; the pre-registered W1 rule (KEEP / WEAK / TIE / NONE).

**Results table (12-cell scaled effect MAE; lower is better).**

**Claims the evidence supports.** (i) A world model trained on the factual stream ignores interventions; training on
paired branches teaches the first week's effect (−0.075 vs a step-matched control, three seeds) and only that.
(ii) On confounded observational data, the propensity-weighted contrast removes more than half of the naive estimator's
excess error, so the split's confounding is real and measurable. (iii) Two established time-varying treatment-effect
methods (CRN, CT), adapted with every deviation documented and trained under a fixed budget, do not beat "no effect" on
either split; they over-react in the confounded direction. (iv) No method learns the 30-day sign reversal of the food
effects or the split timing; a hazard head can be calibrated to the base rate but carries little information.
(v) "Predict no effect" is a strong baseline at 30 days, which any benchmark of long-horizon intervention effects
should report.

**Claims it does not support.** That CRN's or CT's balancing does or does not help (their kept checkpoints have weak
balancing and a small fraction of the authors' steps); that counterfactual data in general beats observational data
(O3 confounds data volume, worlds, initialization and the loss); that these results transfer beyond one simulator,
one scene and single interventions; anything about larger budgets.

**Limitations.** One scene; one cast per world; 400 train worlds per split; small IPW effective sample sizes; daily
tokens for CRN / CT vs hourly M1 inputs; CRN / CT under fixed budgets with their implementations' speed limiting CT;
no rounding of counts for CRN / CT; one validation-chosen configuration per method; GPU reproducibility not measured.

**What is left before submission.** A budget-matched rerun of CRN and CT with α's schedule fitted to the budget and a
faster attention (post hoc today), rounding of counts in their rollouts, a larger observational split (several casts
per world or more worlds) so balancing has data, and at least one more scene.
