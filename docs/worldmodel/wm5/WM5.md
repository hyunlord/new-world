## 2. The obs2 split (Part 1)

**Why.** WM-4's observational split had one act per world, 400 train worlds and IPW effective sample sizes of 44–55: a
reviewer could say balancing had nothing to work with. obs2 has three times the worlds (1,200 train, 200 val, 240 test),
a **sequential multi-act policy** (up to five acts per world, ≥ 8 days apart, each decision read from the trajectory
already changed by the earlier acts), two reference rows per evaluation world and two noise replicates per test family.

**The policy** (METRICS.md part a): WM-4's frozen terciles and every daily probability doubled (gift 0.060 / 0.016 /
0.004, spoil 0.004 / 0.016 / 0.060 for the food-per-person terciles low / middle / high; rot 0.024 / 0.008 by the wood
tercile), its own random stream, a refractory week after each act, at most five acts.

**Recording in rounds.** Round 0 recorded the 1,640 factual worlds; round k re-recorded every world that acted in round
k − 1 with all its acts so far, and the policy continued from the row after its last act. Rounds 1–5 recorded 1,587 /
1,318 / 757 / 265 / 39 worlds; round 6 found no new act. Every round's episodes equal the previous round's on every row
up to the new act (5 × all OK) and carry every earlier act; replaying the policy on a world's final episode reproduces
every round's acts and row probabilities (1,640 / 1,640), so the propensity of every decision row is known.

**Realized.** 3,966 acts, **2.42 per world**, **3.2 % of worlds never acted** (GOAL.md expected 2–3 and ≈ 3 %); 39.9 %
gift, 39.9 % spoil, 20.1 % rot; **60.0 % of acts had an earlier act in the world's history**. The confounding is WM-4's:
in the low-food tercile 80.9 % of acts are gifts and 5.3 % spoils; in the high-food tercile 4.3 % gifts and 72.9 %
spoils. **IPW effective sample sizes**: 396–445 treated per act at 7 days and 126–141 at 30 days (WM-4: 44–55), from
1,163 / 1,174 / 572 treated units at 7 days, more than half of them with an earlier act.

**Families.** For every val / test world at c1 (first act, or day 30) and c2 (second act, or c1 + 14 days): `pre` (the
acts before c) and the three arms (`pre` + the act at c), forked at c; test families with two replicates. 880
families, 7,080 manifest episodes; `fam-check` 880 / 880 (every member equals the observed episode on rows < c and
carries exactly `pre` + its act; `none_r0` = the round-0 factual in all 880; `pre_r0` = a fresh unforked recording in 3
sampled test families). The first `fam-check` failed on a checker bug (DECISIONS L1.5). obs2 test: 240 worlds × 2
references × 3 acts = 1,440 primary cases (replicate 0) and their 1,440 replicate-1 twins for the floor.

**What the test asks.** The truth shows WM-2's 30-day reversal again: a gift's food effect is +21.7 at 7 days and −17.3
at 30; a spoil's −78.0 and +32.8; per-world spread is large (gift 7-day std 41.2).

- **R4.** All eight primary runs completed their plans; no plan was reduced; no run hit the safety cap.


## 6. Q2 — is the benchmark discriminative? The headroom ladder (F1, F2, E1, E2)

**Yes, the benchmark has room; the methods do not reach it.** The ladder (12-cell scaled MAE; Δ with world-bootstrap
95 % CI):

| split | lag | floor | `ridge_priv` (diagnostic) | `ridge_obs` | `zero` | `zero` − floor | `zero` − `ridge_obs` | `ridge_obs` − `ridge_priv` | `ridge_priv` − floor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v2 | 7 d | 0.187 | 0.434 | 0.480 | 0.612 | +0.425 [+0.374, +0.483] | +0.133 [+0.088, +0.178] | +0.045 [+0.028, +0.063] | +0.247 [+0.213, +0.282] |
| v2 | 30 d | 0.267 | 0.590 | 0.602 | 0.487 | +0.220 [+0.114, +0.327] | −0.115 [−0.155, −0.073] | +0.012 [−0.005, +0.031] | +0.323 [+0.227, +0.411] |
| obs2 | 7 d | 0.058 | 0.391 | 0.537 | 0.217 | +0.159 [+0.144, +0.175] | −0.319 [−0.335, −0.302] | +0.146 [+0.131, +0.159] | +0.333 [+0.318, +0.349] |
| obs2 | 30 d | 0.095 | 0.497 | 0.514 | 0.188 | +0.094 [+0.070, +0.116] | −0.326 [−0.342, −0.309] | +0.017 [−0.011, +0.043] | +0.402 [+0.370, +0.436] |

- **The floor is far below `zero` on both splits and at both lags** (F1, F2): the per-world effect is mostly fixed by the
  state at the act; post-fork noise is a small part. On v2 the floor equals √2 × WM-2's between-replicate spread exactly
  (0.187 = √2 × 0.132; 0.267 = √2 × 0.189). It is a reference point, not a hard ceiling: for continuous symmetric noise
  it over-states the noise-limited error by ≈ √2, and in sparse count cells (deaths at 30 d) it can be worse than `zero`
  (review round 1, m4).
- **E1 v2 7 d: `ridge_obs` beats `zero` (WEAK, ratio 0.783; a deterministic one-run side is capped at WEAK by the W1
  rule).** Post hoc (review M1) it only ties the act's mean effect — vs `arm_mean` −0.017 [−0.037, +0.002], vs `an5_arm`
  −0.006 [−0.037, +0.025] — so linearly the observable state adds nothing measurable beyond the arm-level mean. The
  hidden state does add per-world information (`ridge_priv` vs `arm_mean` −0.063 [−0.082, −0.043], vs `an5_arm` −0.051
  [−0.080, −0.022], post hoc), but `ridge_priv` stays 0.247 above the floor: most of the headroom is not linear in the
  state at the act, observable or hidden. At 30 d (E1 NONE) the regressions lose to `zero`, as `arm_mean` does: they fit
  squared error (a mean) while the metric rewards a median near zero for these skewed, mostly-zero effects.
- **E1 obs2 (NONE at both lags) measures v2→obs2 transfer, not what obs2's state carries** (review M2): every v2
  training case of a gift or a rot sits at one cast row (490), obs2's test cases at 61 rows, and no obs2 gift or rot case
  falls on a v2 training row (spoils 20.4 %). Four cells chose λ = 0.01 on v2 val (same rows as train), and the
  unregularized fits extrapolate badly (`rw120|alive|7d` 1.535 vs `zero` 0.036). F2's ridge rungs say nothing about
  observable vs hidden state on obs2.
- **E2.** Every primary CRN / CT model is NONE against `ridge_obs` on v2; on obs2 the comparison is against a regression
  that transfers badly, so the obs2 E2 WEAK / KEEP verdicts of the M1 models mostly reflect `ridge_obs`'s failure there.
  `wm3_full` ties `ridge_obs` on v2 at 7 d (+0.016 [−0.035, +0.081]) and beats it at 30 d (WEAK). M1b-obs2 is KEEP against `ridge_obs` on obs2 at both lags, which again says more about the regression's transfer than about M1b-obs2 (R1 TIE with `zero`).


## 7. Q3 — does more observational data help? (D1)

**Barely.** On the obs2 test, three times more observational data and several acts per world do not make the
obs-trained models better in a way that matters:
- CT-obs2 vs WM-4's CT-obs: 7 d −0.006 [−0.021, +0.009] (TIE, as kept) / −0.012 [−0.026, +0.004] (TIE, CT-obs
  re-scored rounded); 30 d +0.105 [+0.076, +0.135] (**NONE**: worse). CT-obs2's errors are the authors' schedule plus
  more data; WM-4's CT-obs was an early checkpoint, which on these data is the better one at 30 days, so this
  comparison mixes data size with schedule and checkpoint.
- M1b-obs2 vs WM-4's M1b-obs: 7 d −0.003 [−0.005, −0.001], 30 d −0.004 [−0.006, −0.001] (**WEAK** at both lags): a real but tiny gain (≈ 1.5–2 % of the score), still at `zero` (R1 TIE, R2 NONE by +0.005). M1b-obs2 did not converge
  (§3); with three times the worlds and the same 8,000-step cap each world is seen less often, so this may understate
  what more data can do.
- None of the obs-trained models or WM-2's / WM-3's references beats `zero` on obs2 (M1b-obs2: TIE; M1b-act 0.215–0.225
  at 7 d, `wm3_full` 0.218–0.257, M1b-obs 0.218 / 0.221; `zero` 0.217).


## 8. Q4 — how much of WM-4's CRN / CT deficit was rounding? (D2)

**Almost none.** Re-scoring WM-4's eight kept CRN / CT runs with M1's readout rounding on their own test splits changes
the seed-averaged 12-cell scores by −0.006 to +0.018, every interval containing 0 (obs: CRN +0.001 [−0.007, +0.010] at
7 d, −0.003 [−0.008, +0.002] at 30 d; CT −0.006 [−0.014, +0.003], −0.004 [−0.010, +0.003]; v2: CRN +0.003 [−0.085,
+0.099], −0.004 [−0.029, +0.021]; CT +0.018 [−0.060, +0.098], +0.016 [−0.013, +0.043]). The deaths cells barely move
(WM-4's runs, e.g. `wm4:ct_v2_s0` 7 d 1.409 → 1.549, `wm4:crn_v2_s0` 30 d 4.187 → 4.183): the two rollouts of CRN / CT differ by whole deaths,
not by fractions that rounding would remove. WM-4's unrounded predictions are reproduced exactly by the re-score's
unrounded readout for seven runs; `ct_obs_s1` differs at the 1e-4 relative level (GPU nondeterminism, DECISIONS L4.2).

## 9. What changed relative to WM-4's conclusions

- **"CRN and CT were not trained properly" — answered: it was not the training.** WM-4's conclusion (they do not beat
  `zero` on either split) stands with the authors' full schedule, the last checkpoint and rounding; on v2 the fully
  trained runs are further behind (post hoc).
- **Rounding was not the cause** (D2: every interval contains 0).
- **"The split was too small for balancing" — answered: not the size.** obs2's IPW effective sample sizes at 7 days are
  396–445 per act against WM-4's 44–55; IPW recovers the mean effects much better than `naive`, yet CRN and CT still over-react in the
  confounded direction and lose to `zero`.
- **"Nothing can beat zero" — partly withdrawn.** The floor lies far below `zero` on every split (headroom 0.425 / 0.220
  on v2, 0.159 / 0.094 on obs2), so the benchmark has room; on v2 at 7 days a ridge regression beats `zero`, but only up
  to the act's mean effect (as `arm_mean` / `an5_arm` do); hidden state adds a little (post hoc). WM-4's statement "no
  model trained on any data beats `zero`" remains true of the learned models on the observational splits.
- **Unchanged:** world models barely respond to the act; `wm3_full` (paired branches) is the best learned model on v2 at
  7 days and ties `ridge_obs` there. Post hoc, on the 30-day sign reversal of the mean food effect: for spoils no learned
  dynamics model reproduces it (CT-obs2 has the right sign at a fraction of the size, +12.6 / +23.4 vs +32.8); for
  gifts it is mixed by seed (CRN-obs2 s1 −15.9 vs true −17.3, s0 +12.2; CT-obs2 s0 −6.6, s1 +5.2), and a right mean
  is not a right per-world prediction (CRN-obs2 s1's error in that cell 0.822 vs `zero` 0.279). Why the sign reverses
  is not analysed here.


## 10. Design caveats

- **One simulator, one scene, three acts at one amount.** obs2's policy is synthetic: known propensities, a refractory
  week and a cap a real decision-maker would not have.
- **obs2 cases inherit the policy's timing** (c1 / c2 are the policy's act rows or fixed fallbacks), as WM-4's obs cases
  did; the v2 and obs2 tests ask different questions and are not pooled.
- **The direct regressions are linear and trained on v2's counterfactual labels**; on obs2 they extrapolate outside
  v2's cast-row support (§6), so they bound nothing there.
- **The floor is a reference, not a ceiling** (§6).
- **CRN / CT at daily resolution, one configuration each** (chosen on WM-4's smaller obs split), two seeds; M1 at hourly
  resolution. CRN / CT's primary checkpoint is the last step (the authors' practice), which on these data is not their
  best factual checkpoint: CT's `val_roll` is lowest at its first or second evaluation (CT-v2 at steps 790 / 1,580 of
  11,850) and the CRN-obs2 s0 decoder's last evaluation spiked (1.372 vs 0.63–0.73 before; s1's last 0.787 vs its best
  0.769) — the secondary checkpoints are reported beside the primary ones.
- **CT-obs2's cost check was measured three times** (DECISIONS L4.4); the plan used is the third, clean measurement's.
- **obs2 trainings used an observed-only manifest** (DECISIONS L1.4), identical in its observed entries to the final one.
- **GPU nondeterminism**: one WM-4 CT run re-scored to 1e-4 relative differences (DECISIONS L4.2); GPU reproducibility was
  not measured.
- **An SSH stall** (2.1 h) is recorded as a pause; DGX jobs ran on.
- **WM-4 run names collide with WM-5's** (`crn_v2_s0` …); `wm4:` prefixes mark WM-4's runs here.
