## 1. Summary

- **Q0 (audit): no bug.** The data, the action encoding and the rollout harness are aligned (0 structural mismatches in
  3,592 train + val cases; the harness reproduces the model's own step in 3 × 306 cases). WM-2's wrong-signed one-step
  response is the trained models': they see the action but answer an hour after a +238 food gift with +6 / −12 / +18.
- **Q1 (primary): no.** Fine-tuning M1b-act on cast windows with a paired-difference loss and a hazard head does not
  predict the 30-day effects better than "no effect": V1 `wm3_full` 0.511 vs `zero` 0.487, Δ +0.024 [+0.014, +0.040],
  **NONE** (WM-2's M1b-act: 0.501). At 7 days it ties the best baseline (V2: 0.496 vs `an5_arm` 0.485, Δ +0.011
  [−0.044, +0.074], **TIE**), which WM-2's models did not come near (0.559–0.650): post hoc, the fine-tuning cut the
  7-day effect error by 0.096 [0.067, 0.122] against the checkpoints it started from.
- **Q2 (hazard): no.** The hazard head turns "never predicts a split" into a split predicted too early and badly
  calibrated: V3 split shift **TIE** with `an5_arm` (11.0 vs 11.4 days, the shift predicted ≈ 0), V4 split timing
  **NONE** (expected day MAE 46.0 vs `ridge_day` 29.2); P(split within 30 days) 0.76–0.99 predicted vs 0.41 observed (validation).
- **Q3 (ablation): the 7-day gain is the objective's, not more training's** (post hoc on the 7-day cells:
  `wm3_full_s0` vs `wm3_ctrl_s0` −0.091 [−0.108, −0.073]; cast windows alone `wm3_os_s0` vs `wm3_ctrl_s0` −0.005
  [−0.043, +0.043]); on V5's pre-registered 30-day cells the three configurations are within 0.011 of each other
  (`full` vs `ctrl` +0.006 [+0.000, +0.012] NONE; `os` vs `ctrl` TIE; `full` vs `os` TIE).
- **Factual guard (V6): no cost at the pre-registered 168 h.** Every WM-3 run is at least as good as its init on raw
  T2 168 h (−0.002 to −0.007); at 24 h (reported, not judged) the `full` and `os` runs are 1–3 % worse than their inits
  (+0.0014 to +0.0036, CIs above 0; the selection allowed `val_t2` 24 h up to 5 % worse), `ctrl` 2 % better.

## 2. The audit (Part 1): a training problem, not a bug

WM-2's diagnostic showed a one-step response with the wrong sign (an hour after a gift of 240 food the models predicted
+6 or −12). Two different causes were possible: a misalignment somewhere between the recorder, the training pairs, the
action encoding and the rollout harness (a bug), or models that did not learn the mechanics (a training problem).
`wm3/audit.py` checked the links separately on every train and validation case (3,592 cases; no test family opened;
AUDIT.md has the tables):

- **A1 data** — in the arm member's prepared training arrays the action is non-zero exactly at the cast row and the
  arm's kind column, value requested / `ACTION_SCALE`; the partner's actions are zero; the two members' inputs are equal
  on every row ≤ c; their stock difference at c is exactly 0. **0 structural mismatches.** The recorded jump arm − partner
  at c + 1 over the signed applied amount lies in [0.5, 1.5] in 3,588 of 3,592 cases (the 4 others are `sp120L` casts
  at the earliest cast row with an extra drop of the store in the same hour, below the 0.5 % inspection threshold);
  `rot_wood` is exactly 1 everywhere, food arms within ±10–30 % because agents eat, gather or stop foraging in that hour.
- **A2 one-step response** — the WM-2 checkpoints' teacher-forced predicted change at the cast row, arm minus partner
  (inputs that differ only in that one action entry): validation food jump +6.3 / −12.0 / +18.1 (s0 / s1 / s2) for a
  true +237.7 (`gf240E`), −22.9 / −43.6 / −41.6 for −225.3 (`sp240L`), −19.7 / −66.7 / −54.4 for a true 0 (`rw120E`,
  whose wood response is −3.2 / −1.1 / −4.0 for −120); correlation over cases between predicted and true jumps
  between −0.36 and +0.25.
- **A3 harness** — the rollout harness's lag-1 difference equals `Stepper.next_state` applied to A2's outputs in every
  one of 3 × 306 validation cases (largest deviation 0.6 % of a 1e-4-relative tolerance); a stub model whose food
  change is the action itself comes back exactly (+240 / −240 / −120 / 0).

**Decision (`K1.decision`): A1 and A3 clean.** The action reaches the model at the right row with the right value, the
recorded jump is at c → c + 1 and the harness reproduces the model's step. WM-2's models answer the action with a small part of
the recorded jump (3–8 % of the gift's recorded jump, s1 with the wrong sign; 1–3 % of the `rot_wood` wood jump, 10–19 % (`sp240L`) and 23–37 % (`sp120L`) of the spoils'). A hypothesis for why (section 12): a cast is 1/336 of the teacher-forced loss in ≈ 10 % of the
training windows and WM-2's selection barely rewards an effect — but raising the share of cast windows alone (`wm3_os`)
does not change the response, so this is at most part of the explanation. No erratum to WM-2.

## 3. The idea tested: paired branches with shared noise

Dataset v2's families give every intervention a **partner**: the factual member of the same world and replicate,
identical up to the cast row and sharing the post-fork noise stream, so arm − partner after c is the intervention's
effect on that trajectory, without independent noise. WM-3 fine-tunes each WM-2 M1b-act checkpoint (`M1TFH`: M1TF plus
a hazard head on the same final state, bit-identical to M1TF on mu / logvar / fire when loaded with an M1TF state dict —
a unit test on CPU, and the step-0 validation score of every run equalled its init's)
with:

- **cast windows**: half of each batch is windows whose rollout starts exactly at a cast (the action at position 167,
  the last teacher-forced row), drawn uniformly over the 2,980 train cases;
- **pair loss** (weight 1): the cast window and its partner are rolled 168 steps from the same state and penalized on
  the error of the *difference*, (cur_A − cur_F) − (true_A − true_F), feature-weighted as WM-2's rollout loss;
- **hazard head** (weight 10): a logistic head predicting a fission in the next hour at every teacher-forced row; from it
  an expected restricted split day E = Σ P_k day(c + k) + S_K day(last row) replaces WM-2's event-based day (which never
  fired: WM-2's rolled `fissions_total` never rose by 0.5);
- **effect-aware checkpoint selection**: among checkpoints whose validation T2 24 h is within 5 % of the init's, the one
  with the lowest validation effect error (24 cells: 4 outcomes × 3 arms × 7 and 30 days, replicate 0), the init
  included.

Two ablations share the selection: `wm3_ctrl` (32 plain windows, WM-2's objective only — is any gain just more
training under an effect-aware selection?) and `wm3_os` (16 plain + 16 cast windows, no pair or hazard loss — cast
windows alone).

What changed and what did not:

- **The 7-day effect is now learned on average.** `wm3_full_s1` / `_s2` predict the mean 7-day food effect of all three
  arms to within ≈ 20 % (`wm3_full_s0`, kept at step 500: the `rot_wood` mean, half of the spoil, a sixth of the gift);
  WM-2's models, `wm3_ctrl` and `wm3_os` predict 0–16 % of the spoil and gift effects (their `rot_wood` 7-day numbers

  `deaths` and `settlements_alive` tied.

  costs ≈ 90 food after 7 days but leaves the store ≈ 106 *above* the factual after 30 days (presumably through the agents' foraging, which A1 shows reacting within the hour; not

The hazard head now predicts splits — too many and too early. A fission happens in ≈ 3 × 10⁻⁴ of the rows; the heads
of the kept checkpoints put ≈ 2–6 × 10⁻³ per hour on the factual rollouts (1 − (1 − h)^720 = 0.76–0.99). The miscalibration falls with the kept step
across the three seeds (500 → 2,000: 0.99 → 0.76 predicted vs 0.41 observed; step and seed are confounded, n = 3), so a
likely reason is that a freshly initialized head at
lr 1e-4 had not reached the base rate by the step the effect criterion kept: the selection never looked at the
hazard. The head is off-rate on real states too, not only on drifted rollouts: a reviewer's validation check (REVIEW.md, not a
committed run file; 40 factual episodes, teacher-forced) gave a mean hazard of 7.3 / 3.5 / 2.5 × 10⁻³ per row (s0 / s1 / s2) against an observed
3.6 × 10⁻⁴. The ranking is weakly informative — the observed rate is higher in the top quantile bin than in the bottom
one (0.13–0.29 in the lowest, 0.65–0.68 in the highest; not monotone in between for s0 and s2) — but the level is wrong, so E is 34–49 days early on average and V4 is lost by
≈ 17 days. The expected-day split shift is near 0: per-arm means −0.02 … −1.27 days on the two W4 arms (−4.35 … +0.61 over all
four arms and seeds), right in direction for
`sp240L` (true −20 days) and wrong for `gf240E` (true +4.4): it ties `an5_arm` (V3), WM-2's validation choice, which on
the test families is slightly worse than predicting no shift (post hoc: per-world 11.41 vs `zero` 11.08 days; cell
average 12.49 vs 12.21); `wm3_full`'s 11.02 is no better than `zero`.

## 9. The ablation

On the pre-registered 30-day cells (V5) the three `_s0` configurations are within 0.011: neither the objective nor the
cast windows changed the 30-day error. On the 7-day cells (post hoc, same bootstrap):

| comparison (7 d cells) | Δ [95 % CI] |
|---|---|
| `wm3_full_s0` vs `wm3_ctrl_s0` (objective + cast windows vs more training) | −0.091 [−0.108, −0.073] |
| `wm3_os_s0` vs `wm3_ctrl_s0` (cast windows alone) | −0.005 [−0.043, +0.043] |
| `wm3_full_s0` vs `wm3_os_s0` (pair + hazard loss beyond cast windows) | −0.085 [−0.136, −0.049] |
| `wm3_ctrl_s0` vs `wm2_m1bact_v19_s0` (more training under the effect-aware selection) | −0.027 [−0.033, −0.021] |

So the 7-day gain comes from what `full` adds to `os`: the pair loss, the hazard term (presumably too weak to move
the trunk much; not measured) and 16 partner windows per step that also enter WM-2's teacher-forced and rollout terms (48 against 32
sequences, so `full` vs `os` is not compute-matched either); these are not separated here, the pair loss being the
only one aimed at the effect: putting the casts into the batch is not enough when the loss on them is the same per-feature loss as
everywhere else, and more training under the effect-aware selection buys only a quarter of it. All four are single-seed.

## 10. Design caveats

- **The control is step-matched, not compute-matched**: 32 sequences per step against 48 for `wm3_full` (16 + 16 cast
  + 16 partners); `wm3_os` sees 32. `wm3_ctrl_s0` also hit `max_steps` with its best checkpoint in the last 10 % (not
  converged), so more steps might have helped it — at 7 days it would have needed 3–4 × its gain to reach `wm3_full_s0`.
- **Training rollouts see more context than inference rollouts**: inherited from WM-2's `losses_tf`, a training
  rollout attends to every teacher-forced key ≤ its start (up to 168) plus the appended tokens (up to 335 keys), while
  inference trims the cache to 167; the cast and partner windows inherit this.
- **The hazard gradient into the trunk is small**: fissions are ≈ 3 × 10⁻⁴ of the rows (2.8 × 10⁻⁴ of the sampled
  rows, ≈ 0.9 per 2,881-row episode — fewer than GOAL.md's 1–3 estimate), so the unweighted hazard BCE is 0.001–0.045
  (0.013–0.45 with weight 10) against a total loss between −3.1 and −1.4: the head learns a rate, the trunk gets little signal to represent the approach to a
  split; and the checkpoint selection does not look at the hazard at all (section 8).
- **The pair loss stops at 168 steps**, so the 30-day reversal is outside the objective; the effect-aware selection
  rewards 30-day error, which may have kept checkpoints where the 7-day gain was smaller than it could be.
- **The selection reads validation replicate 0 of the three W3 arms** on 720-row rollouts; the test rules read both
  replicates and the full horizon (shifts, the two W4 arms) or 168 / 720 rows (effects, the three W3 arms).
- **One trained seed for `ctrl` and `os`**; every V5 and post-hoc ablation statement is single-seed.

## 11. Not in WM-3 (WM-4)

Literature baselines for counterfactual prediction over time — CRN (Bica et al. 2020) and the Causal Transformer
(Melnychuk et al. 2022) — are not run here. Their contribution is a balancing representation that removes time-varying
confounding (treatments assigned depending on the history). In dataset v2 every arm is cast on every world by a fixed
rule (fixed tick, fixed amount), so there is no treatment-selection bias for them to correct and they would reduce to a
sequence model like M1b-act. WM-4 adds an observational split with confounded assignment (casts triggered by the
state), where those methods belong.

## 12. What a paper can claim

**The strongest honest claim:** in a deterministic agent-based world with paired, noise-sharing intervention branches,
a learned world model that predicts the factual future better than the calendar baselines (WM-2 W1) ignores the effect of rare interventions — not because of a data or evaluation bug (Part 1); raising the share of cast
windows alone does not change that (`os` ≈ `ctrl`), so "a vanishing share of the loss" is at most part of the
explanation (a hypothesis) — and a paired-difference objective on those branches teaches it the first week of the
effect (post hoc — the pre-registered 7-day rule V2 is a TIE: the 7-day effect error falls by 16 % against the same
checkpoints, −0.096 [−0.122, −0.067]; the gain is the paired configuration's — pair loss, hazard term and partner
windows, not separated, and `full` vs `os` is not compute-matched (48 vs 32 sequences) — not more training's or the cast
windows' alone) without costing factual accuracy at 168 h (V6; 1–3 % worse at 24 h). It still does not beat case-based
baselines at 7 days (V2 TIE) or "no effect" at 30 days (V1 NONE), where the true effect reverses sign (presumably
through the agents' behaviour; not inspected).

Backed by: AUDIT.md (A1, A3: 0 mismatches; A2: the one-step response); V2 TIE and V1 NONE; V6; the post-hoc 7-day
comparisons of sections 6 and 9 (labelled as post hoc; one seed for the ablations, three for `full` vs WM-2).

Not supported:
- "The model predicts intervention effects better than simple baselines" — V1 NONE, V2 TIE.
- "The hazard head predicts splits" — V4 NONE, calibration 0.76–0.99 vs 0.41; V3's tie is with a baseline that
  is slightly worse than predicting no shift on the test families.
- "Paired training fixes the one-step response" — the 1-hour response is still 8–32 % of the true jump; the gain is in
  the rollout over the first day.
- Any 30-day claim: no configuration learned the reversal.

Next experiment for each open claim:
- **30-day effects**: extend the pair rollout to 720 steps (or a curriculum to it) so the reversal is in the objective,
  and add the arm-conditioned case lookup (`an5_arm`) as a residual target; test against `zero` and `arm_mean` exactly as
  V1.
- **Beating `an5_arm` at 7 days**: the food cells are lost on case-to-case variation; condition the pair loss on the
  world state (or add a per-case effect head trained on the paired difference) and re-test V2 with three seeds of a
  compute-matched control.
- **Hazard**: train the head to the base rate before fine-tuning (or select hazard checkpoints on validation Brier
  score), weight positives by the window's distance to the split, and re-test V3 / V4.
- **One-step mechanics**: give the action a direct additive path to the mean change of the store it acts on (the model
  knows the cast amount), and check A2 again.
- **WM-4**: CRN and the Causal Transformer on an observational split with confounded, state-triggered casts, where
  their balancing has something to correct; M1b-act + pair loss as the sequence-model reference.
