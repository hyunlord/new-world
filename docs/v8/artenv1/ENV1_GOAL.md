## Why
Route I gave the colony its people (34 looks × 10 frames). The simulation meanwhile grew walls, doors, floors, dug rock, bedrolls, five workshops, clay and pottery, ground axes, spears, hides and clothes, meat, fords, wolves and deer (M3–M5); the 05 environment set (TRIAL2b, 35 assets) predates all of it. M-ART2 will install the art into the game; before it does, two questions need answers the owner can see:
1. **Coverage:** is every simulated thing drawable in 05, consistently?
2. **Readability:** the current debug renderer reads well *because* the ground is dark and calm and only people, buildings and the HUD stand out (owner, 2026-10-23: "림월드·드워프 포트리스 생각나고 시뮬 보기 좋다"). The risk is that illustrated ground and dense detail make the simulation harder to read, especially zoomed out, at night and in winter. TRIAL2 already showed a busy generated meadow burying people. So the art must be applied with restraint: **ink detail on things and people, calm toned-down ground, markers when zoomed out** — and that has to be demonstrated on real colony data, not a staged board.

# 5. PART 4 — The rehearsal compositor (`art/compositor/`)
A Python renderer that draws a **real colony scene** with the adopted art. No game code; the scene comes from the simulation's own text view plus a declared overlay:
1. **Scene source A (real):** build `worldsim_probe` at `v8/wip` HEAD in a separate read-only worktree, run `worldsim_probe --scene colony --seeds 0..1 --ascii --tick T --commands tools/v8/soak_script.json --out scene_T.txt --people-json people_T.json` at T = day 6 noon, day 6 02:00, day 30 noon (winter if the calendar says so; otherwise pick the first winter noon), and parse the glyph grid with the legend in `snapshot_schema.md` "Text renderer glyphs" (terrain, zones, logs, sites, hearth, huts, trees, stumps, people awake/asleep/children, carriers). Record the exact commands and the probe commit in the scene file.
2. **Scene source B (declared overlay):** the text view does not draw M3–M5 things yet. Write `scenes/m5_overlay.json` placing, on the same map near the camp, what the M5 screen set shows (`docs/images/m5_contact_sheet.png`): a 7×5 walled house with door and bedrolls, a walled storehouse with mixed items, a dug pocket, the pottery bench, grinding stone, drying rack with a hide, a clay bank, a ford, 2 wolves approaching and 3 deer in the forest, 4 people wearing hide wraps, 2 carrying logs, 1 chopping. Mark every overlay object as declared (not simulated) in the render's legend.
3. **People:** route I frames by look; look chosen from `people_T.json` (sex, life stage) and the overlay (top, hair; default per agent id modulo the look list); activity → frame per HANDOVER's frame plan; foot anchor and contact shadow per HANDOVER.
4. **Zooms:** 1× (64-px tiles), ½× (32), ¼× (16, droppable). **LOD rule:** at ½× and below, people are drawn as a marker — a filled circle 10 px (½×) / 6 px (¼×) in the person's top colour with a 1-px ink ring, a small activity glyph (axe, log, zz) at ½× only; animals as a coloured triangle (wolf red, deer tan). Document the thresholds; also render ½× **without** LOD for comparison.
5. **Ground restraint:** a tone cap applied to ground tiles only: mean luminance ≤ 0.40, luminance std ≤ 60 % of the person anchor's, saturation mean ≤ 0.30 (measure and log per tile); objects and people uncapped.
6. **Conditions:** day, night (hearth light), winter, drought (droppable).
7. Output per scene × zoom × condition a PNG at the game window size (1600×900, centred on the camp), plus the HUD strip cropped from the current renderer's screenshot pasted at the top so the comparison is fair.
