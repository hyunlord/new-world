## Projection, anchors and visibility

Every render is 1600×900. The authentic unchanged HUD occupies y=0…111; camp tile `[91,101]` projects to `(800,506)`. Tile sizes are 64, 32 and 16 pixels at 1×, ½× and ¼×. Each 1× or ½× render uses the exact scene-and-zoom HUD from `reference/current_*_hud.png`. The ¼× diagnostic borrows the corresponding half-zoom HUD because a quarter baseline was not captured. Cross-condition diagnostics retain the source scene's authentic clock/season text; only the matched day/noon, night/02:00 and winter/noon pairs enter readability testing.

Route I canvases remain 80×112 upright and 112×80 sleeping. The foot anchor is `(40,104)` or `(56,72)` and maps to tile center plus 11 pixels south at 1×. The separate contact shadow is 28×8 pixels at 1×. Text uses the existing macOS Arial font; a non-macOS reproduction must explicitly supply the same font to reproduce annotations byte-for-byte. S frames are used; frame mapping is awake→idle, sleep→sleep, carry→carry, chop→work_a. No limbs, palette or accepted frame pixels are repainted.

Existing environment assets use their recorded `anchor_point`, including the off-center tree root anchor. New footprint canvases use the center unless their manifest provides `anchor_px`. Standing animals use `(48,72)` in 96×80; dead animals would use `(48,56)` in 96×64. The bedroll stays at its specified 64×128, 1×2 art footprint; native bed occupancy itself is one tile. The art size is not silently changed to match either native occupancy or smaller visible bodies inside padded person canvases.

Real stockpile zones use a code-generated thin translucent outline; they do not become invented physical floors. Blueprint sites use the actual finished wall/fixture sprite with a pale outline and 25% wash. Completed native wall topology includes adjoining planned wall cells for consistent edge direction. Foreground objects and people share one world-y depth sort. People are last only at an identical x/y point. Initial preview found a declared actor exactly on a real lean-to roof; before Round 0 the declared C actor moved to camp offset `(-4,0)`, the second carrier to `(2,-3)` and A to `(-5,3)`. A tile-collision audit additionally moved the dug pocket away from two verified native ford cells and moved A off a river tile before Round 0. Source positions and the changed declared positions are both retained in evidence.

Visibility evidence distinguishes anchors inside the map from retained figure pixels. A separate ownership raster tracks alpha≥128 figure/marker pixels through later object/person occlusion, then excludes HUD and footer. Shadows, letters and activity glyphs do not inflate figure visibility. Each person records original opaque pixels, retained opaque pixels, source position, screen foot and actual alpha≥16 figure bounds. A/B/C letters sit just above those visible bounds, not above transparent canonical padding. This resolves the preview's ambiguous B label and supplies neutral annotation coordinates to the blind-packet builder.

## Ground and conditions: settings locked before Round 0

The registered maxima remain luminance mean≤0.40, luminance standard deviation≤0.60×the locked person anchor and saturation mean≤0.30. The initial compositor chooses stricter targets before any blind review: mean≤0.32, standard deviation≤0.25×person and saturation≤0.22. `tone.py` keeps a 10% rounding margin inside each target. Luminance is Rec.709 weighted encoded sRGB, not a linear-light claim. Person reference pixels exclude transparent and magenta background pixels.

Terrain maps by exact glyph: meadow→dark grass, forest→forest floor, hill/rock/cave→hill rock, river→river bank, and designated packed ground→packed earth. River texture receives one logged tile-wide RGB multiplier `[0.55,0.85,1.30]` before the normal caps; this distinguishes water using a global transform. No local texture editing or quantization is used. Packed-earth and dug-floor overlays receive the same tone treatment as terrain.

The order is resize → condition transform → optional generated snow composite → global contrast/chroma cap → foreground → global night value → unchanged HUD. Winter desaturates the ground, then composites the once-generated snow texture with alpha capped at 153/255 (60%) before recapping. Drought mixes ground toward ochre. Night blue-shifts ground only, drops the whole scene's RGB value by 45%, and linearly restores value inside five tiles of a hearth. People, items and buildings retain their hues; only global night value changes them.

Each render records the source and transformed metrics of every used tile asset, preserving the source asset's failure/candidate status separately from its transformed numeric result; winter records the snow source status as well. It also measures every visible ground-only tile crop after all season/snow/cap/hearth-light operations and before foreground/HUD. Both registered and stricter target results are retained. A registered final-tile failure raises an error. This avoids assuming that a pre-light texture measurement proves the final lit ground.

Their fill is sampled from their adopted look's actual S/idle torso pixels, not an ID palette. The sampler uses the opaque body bounds, central x=25–75% and y=45–70%, alpha≥128, maximum RGB channel 50…225, and channel medians.
