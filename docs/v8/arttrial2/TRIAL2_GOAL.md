## Why this pass exists

The owner chose **concept 05 (잉크 선화: fine ink line work, two-tone shading, restrained colour wash)** on 2026-10-22 as the single style for the whole game, map and portraits alike, **on one condition: it must be sustainable** — hundreds of assets over years, several eras, generated by a model that drifts, plus procedural overlays drawn in code, must all stay one look. ART-CONCEPT proved the look exists; it did not prove it can be produced at volume. ART-TRIAL (32-px pixel art) burned 1,019 generations mostly on person layers and failed; that must not repeat.

This pass is the rehearsal. It is judged on **production numbers**, not on beauty:
- average tries per accepted asset, per family;
- style-judge pass rate against locked anchors;
- whether a wall autotile set can be derived from one generated material;
- whether seasons can be shader tints instead of regenerated textures;
- whether a person can be a cut-out rig built from generated parts and animated in Godot, with attributes and equipment as part swaps;
- whether code-drawn overlays can sit beside generated assets.

The report ends with a verdict: **05 sustainable / 05 for environment + code-drawn people / fall back to 02**, with the numbers that decide it. The owner decides after.

# 3. PART 0 — Anchors, bible, judge

## 3.1 Anchors (`art/style05/anchors/`)
Pick six accepted 05 originals as the **locked style anchors**: one person (S1 or S2), the hut (S4), the props (S5), the ground quad (S6), the camp scene (S7), the portrait (S8). Commit them at original resolution with a `ANCHORS.md` naming each and what it anchors (line weight, wash density, palette, light). **These files never change.** Every generation in this pass and in production attaches at least two anchors (the family's nearest + the scene).

## 3.2 Style bible v2 (`art/style05/STYLE.md`, English with a Korean summary)
From 05's CONCEPT.md plus what the anchors actually show: medium, line weight in px at 1024² (measure it: mean stroke width from an edge map), wash opacity, the colour range (sample 24 dominant colours from the anchors in Lab, commit as `palette_05.json` + swatch — **for judging, not for quantizing**), light direction, the top-down rule, the front-face cheat, the magenta-background rule for cut-outs, the prompt template with the anchor attachment sentence, and the negative list (isometric, photo, 3D render, pixel art, gradient blur, text).

## 3.3 Style judge (`art/pipeline2/judge.py`, unit-tested)
Two signals, both logged per asset:
1. **Numeric:** (a) Lab colour histogram distance to the anchor palette (Earth mover's or χ²; calibrate the threshold leave-one-out on the six anchors so each anchor passes against the other five); (b) line density: ratio of edge pixels (Canny on luminance) to area, within the anchors' range ± 30 %; (c) wash: mean local saturation within the anchors' range.
2. **Model judge:** the image model in a fresh context, shown two anchors and the candidate, asked for four 1–5 scores (medium, line, light, palette) and one sentence; pass = every score ≥ 4.

An asset **passes** when the numeric checks pass and the model judge passes. Calibrate on the anchors (all must pass) and on three deliberately wrong images (a 02 woodcut asset, a 01 painterly asset, a photo-like generation): all must fail. Record the calibration in `JUDGE_CALIBRATION.md`.

## 3.4 Sidecars
Every accepted asset gets `<name>.json`: prompt, anchors used, try count, operations applied, judge scores, working size, anchor point (for tall objects), and the generation id if the tool returns one.

# 6. PART 3 — The person rig (`art/style05/people/` + `art/proto/`)
## 6.1 Parts (generate at 256–512 px on magenta, attach the person anchor; ≤ 3 tries each)
For **one adult body** seen from above (the game camera): head (with hair placeholder flat `#B0B0B0`), torso, upper arm L/R, forearm L/R, hand L/R, upper leg L/R, lower leg L/R, foot L/R. Generate them as **one labelled parts sheet** ("character parts sheet, each part separated, same lighting") first; if the model cannot keep parts separable, generate the whole figure in a T-pose from above and cut parts in the pipeline (document which).
- Overlays as separate magenta cut-outs, aligned to the torso/head: tops — hide wrap, fur cloak, woven tunic; hair — short, long tied, braided; held items — stone axe, log bundle (shoulder), berry basket; one hat.
- Child and elder bodies: **scale and posture only** (child = 0.75 scale, head 0.9; elder = stoop by −8° torso and a stick item). No separate generation.
## 6.2 Rig (`art/proto/project.godot`, a standalone Godot project; nothing from the game is imported)
- `Skeleton2D` + `Polygon2D`/`Sprite2D` cut-out: bones for torso, head, upper/lower arms, upper/lower legs; parts attached; overlays as child sprites of the bone they belong to; `modulate` channels for skin (flat grey in parts) and the top's main colour area (flat `#C0C0C0` region on the top overlays).
- Animations (AnimationPlayer): `idle` (breathing), `walk` (8-frame cycle, legs and arm swing), `chop` (two-hand swing, 12 frames), `carry` (walk with the log bundle on the shoulder, arms up), `sleep` (lying, static), `knap` (seated hand motion, droppable).
- A `PersonComposer.gd` in the proto: `compose(attrs: Dictionary)` → sets body scale/posture (child/adult/elder), skin tint, hair style, top style and tint (household), held item, animation by state; this is the spec M-ART2 will port into `agent_layer.gd`.
- Rendering at game scale: the proto scene draws at 64-px tiles, camera 1× (person ≈ 56 px), with ground texture family 1 under it.
## 6.3 Readability at game scale
